# Spectral reflectance measurements are widely used in optical-coating assessment, but a measured antireflection spectrum does not uniquely determine layer thickness, refractive-index drift,and porous-layer morphology. These quantities can compensate one another through opticalthickness and effective-medium effects, so inverse coating diagnosis requires both a physicalforward model and a controlled account of non-uniqueness. The study evaluates Bayesian inversediagnosis for a five-layer moth-eye-inspired antireflection coating. The forward model combines Sellmeier dispersion, a Maxwell-Garnett approximation for the porous top layer, and a twoangle transfer-matrix reflectance operator. Two bound-constrained posterior approximationsare compared under matched structure, wavelength grid, parameter bounds, noise realization,and additive Gaussian noise level: annealed sequential Monte Carlo (ASMC) and a physicsconstrained Gaussian variational approximation trained through the differentiable optical model.At 𝜎 = 10−4, ASMC gives lower spectral RMSE (7.68×10−4 versus 6.62×10−3) and lower meanthickness error (0.55% versus 2.45%). At 𝜎 = 5 × 10−3, the Gaussian variational approximationgives lower spectral RMSE (8.10 × 10−3 versus 3.85 × 10−2) and lower mean thickness error(2.46% versus 6.18%). ASMC is faster in both numerical runs, but its average thickness posteriorspread increases from 4.70 nm to 23.65 nm as the noise level rises. The comparison indicates that particle-based sampling and compact variational approximation can lead to different diagnosticconclusions even with the same optical forward model. Posterior spread is therefore reported asan approximation-dependent diagnostic rather than as calibrated uncertainty
